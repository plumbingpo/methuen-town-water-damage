Maintenance Tips for Emergency Water Damage Restoration

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Every member of the household should know where the main water valve is and how it closes.
  • Swap worn hoses feeding the washer, sinks and toilets rather than waiting for a rupture.
  • Any trip beyond a few days is a reason to turn the main supply off before you go.
  • Test your sump pump before the rainy season and consider a battery backup.
  • Important paperwork and valuables belong above floor level, most of all on the lowest story.
  • Know where your electrical panel is so power to wet rooms can be cut safely.

Signs It Is Time for Emergency Water Damage Restoration

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Pooled water is creeping over the floor into neighboring rooms or onto the staircase.
  • Water is coming through a ceiling, light fixture or electrical outlet.
  • Part of your ceiling bows downward because water is pooling on top of it.
  • Water has reached the electrical panel, appliances or power strips on the floor.
  • A water heater tank has failed and is emptying across the floor.
  • Water is still flowing and you cannot find or reach the shutoff valve.
  • Wet carpet, wood flooring or wallboard has stayed saturated longer than a few hours.
