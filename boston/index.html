Water Damage We See Most in Boston

Most water damage calls we take in Boston trace back to a short list of causes. If you can spot these early, you have a good chance of stopping the damage before it spreads.

Pipes: When water freezes inside a line it can crack the metal or plastic, then pour out as soon as things warm up. Lines in exterior walls, garages and unheated attics are the usual trouble spots, depending on the season. In older homes, aging joints and tired connectors sometimes fail with no cold snap involved. Shut off the main valve right away, because an open line can soak several rooms quickly.

Appliances: The thin hoses feeding a washer, dishwasher or ice maker do not last forever. Tanks rust from the inside, so an old water heater may drip for weeks or give out suddenly. These failures do the most harm when the appliance is on an upper floor or above finished living space.

Overflows: It takes only a few minutes for a blocked toilet or a forgotten faucet to flood a bathroom. The water often runs under baseboards and through the ceiling of the room below. If the overflow came from the toilet bowl or a drain, treat it as contaminated and keep children and pets away.

Roof: Missing shingles, cracked flashing and clogged gutters let rain into the attic. By the time a brown ring appears overhead, the attic insulation has often been wet for a while. We dry the framing, insulation and drywall, and a roofer needs to fix the opening so it does not return.

Basement: Once the ground outside is soaked, water finds its way into a basement through wall cracks and the seam where floor meets wall. A sump pump that loses power or jams during a heavy rain lets the pit overflow. Carpet, wall studs and drywall wick moisture upward, which is why finished lower levels take the worst of it.

Mold: Damp drywall, wood and carpet can begin to grow mold in as little as 24 to 48 hours. Many mold jobs we handle in Boston began with an old leak that was wiped up but never dried inside the wall. Have it looked at if you notice a stale odor, bubbling paint or dark specks around an earlier leak.
